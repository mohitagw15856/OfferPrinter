# Fit Memo — Senior Product Analyst at NorthBank

## Snapshot
Alex is a strong analytical and experimentation match for this role, with genuine
end-to-end ownership and A/B testing depth that map directly to NorthBank's needs.
The honest gaps are domain (marketing/retail rather than product/fintech), the dbt
toolchain, and the scale of mentoring — none of them fundamental, but worth being
straight about.

## Requirement-by-requirement
- **4+ years in a data/product/growth analytics role** — Strong match: 4 years across two analyst roles (2020–present).
- **Strong SQL, can model complex data** — Strong match: owns core reporting in PostgreSQL and built datasets replacing a fragile spreadsheet process.
- **Designing and analysing A/B tests / experiments** — Strong match: designed, ran and interpreted 30+ A/B tests, one with a measured 8% conversion lift.
- **Experience with dbt (or strong desire to learn)** — Gap: the CV shows SQL and Looker Studio but no dbt. Meets the "desire to learn" half only.
- **Proficiency in Python or R** — Strong match: uses Python (pandas) for cleaning, joining and modelling data.
- **Defining product metrics and building dashboards** — Partial match: defines metrics and builds dashboards (Looker Studio), but for marketing performance, not product.
- **Excellent communication to non-technical partners** — Strong match: presents monthly readouts to the Head of Marketing and regional managers.
- **Mentoring or leading other analysts** — Partial match: mentored one junior analyst weekly; real but small-scale, not team leadership.
- **Financial services / fintech experience (a plus)** — Gap: experience is in retail and marketing; no fintech shown.
- **Product analytics ownership (onboarding/payments/savings)** — Partial match: owns analytics end to end, but in a marketing area rather than a product surface.

## Honest gaps & how to talk about them
- **dbt** — Don't claim it. Say plainly that your models today are in SQL and
  Looker Studio, that the modelling thinking transfers directly, and that dbt is
  on your near-term learning list (name a small project if you start one).
- **Fintech / financial services domain** — Don't overstate it. Lean on transferable
  rigour: regulated-adjacent retail data, careful experiment design, and comfort
  with messy multi-source data. Frame domain as something you'll ramp on fast.
- **Scale of mentoring** — You've mentored one analyst, not led a team. Be accurate:
  describe what you actually did (weekly SQL and approach reviews) and your appetite
  to do more, rather than implying you've run a team.
- **Product (vs marketing) analytics** — Your ownership model is the same; the
  surface differs. Say so, and point to the experiment-led decision-making that
  carries over cleanly to product.

## Bottom line
A credible, honest fit on the analytical core (SQL, experimentation, communication,
ownership) with clear, nameable gaps in tooling and domain. Worth an application and
an interview, provided the gaps are addressed openly rather than papered over.
